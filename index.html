I hold an M.Sc. in Agricultural and Resource Economics from the University of Saskatchewan, where I work as a Research Assistant on the CanN2ONet project under Dr. Tristan Skolrud and teach as a sessional lecturer. My work asks how offset programs should monitor and pay farmers, and what drives the nitrogen decisions behind N2O emissions.

Research.

Models, data, and the incentives in between.

Working paper

Monitoring, Misreporting, and the Optimal Baseline in Agricultural Offset Programs

A formal model of offset program design with a four-regime monitoring structure and participation thresholds, plus a welfare analysis extending the Horowitz and Just framework to include costly monitoring.

With Tristan Skolrud

CanN2ONet

Nitrogen rates and economic optima on the Prairies

Field-trial response data linked to the 2024 Fertilizer Use Survey. Canola growers apply near the optimum; wheat growers apply more, consistent with protein premiums and risk.

In progress

Do 4R practices reduce crop insurance losses?

Administrative crop insurance records and fertilizer survey data, used to test whether precision nitrogen management lowers insured yield losses in Western Canada.
